Home Water Testing and Filtration Notes: Match Treatment to Lab Data

No single household water filter removes every contaminant, and in-home sales demonstrations rarely substitute for certified laboratory analysis. Starting with a clear water profile keeps you from overpaying for equipment you do not need—or missing a contaminant a basic pitcher filter cannot remove.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) regulates public drinking water systems and requires community utilities to publish an annual Consumer Confidence Report (CCR), while EPA's private well guidance reminds well owners that testing and treatment of private domestic wells are the homeowner's responsibility.

Four steps before requesting equipment quotes

System cost guides, maintenance intervals, and water-problem explainers are available on Water Treatment Pro and its municipal vs well water treatment guide.

  1. Identify your supply source: Public utility water carries residual disinfectant (chlorine or chloramine) and regulated treatment; private wells vary with local aquifer geology and require bacterial and mineral screening.
  2. Use a state-certified lab for health analytes: Bacteria (coliform/E. coli), nitrates, arsenic, PFAS, and lead require certified lab testing rather than quick test strips.
  3. Separate aesthetic from health goals: Hardness scale, iron staining, and chlorine odor affect plumbing and taste; health-based contaminants demand verified certified reduction claims (NSF/ANSI standards).
  4. Factor ongoing consumables into the quote: Replacement cartridges, softener salt, RO membranes, and UV lamps determine five-year ownership cost.
If a certified test shows coliform bacteria, elevated nitrate, arsenic, or lead, consult your local health department or state drinking-water program before relying on an uncertified retail filter.
