Water Hardness Scale and Ion-Exchange Softener Notes

Water hardness measures dissolved calcium and magnesium minerals picked up as water moves through limestone and rock. While hard water is not generally considered a health hazard, it forms chalky mineral scale inside water heaters, showerheads, and dishwashers and reduces soap lather.

USGS general hardness classification

USGS categoryMilligrams per liter (mg/L as CaCO3)Approximate grains per gallon (gpg)
Soft0 to 60 mg/L0 to 3.5 gpg
Moderately hard61 to 120 mg/L3.5 to 7.0 gpg
Hard121 to 180 mg/L7.0 to 10.5 gpg
Very hardMore than 180 mg/LOver 10.5 gpg

What a softener does—and does not—do

Traditional ion-exchange softeners swap calcium and magnesium ions for sodium (or potassium) ions on resin beads, regenerating periodically with brine. They target hardness minerals and small amounts of clear dissolved iron, but they do not disinfect bacteria or remove chlorine, VOCs, or PFAS. Anyone on a sodium-restricted diet should discuss softened drinking water with a clinician or use a dedicated kitchen reverse-osmosis tap.
